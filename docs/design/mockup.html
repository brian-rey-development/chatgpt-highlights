When should I use a Bayesian A/B test instead of a classic frequentist one? Our traffic is pretty low.

With low traffic, a Bayesian test is usually the better fit, mainly because it answers the question you actually care about. A frequentist test tells you how surprising your data would be if there were no difference, while a Bayesian test gives you the probability that variant B is better than A, given the data you have seen.

Why it matters with low traffic

  • No fixed sample size. Frequentist tests require you to commit to a sample size up front, and peeking at results early inflates your false-positive rate.
  • Priors help when data is scarce. An informative prior built from past experiments acts like extra data, so you reach a useful conclusion with fewer visitors.
  • Decisions, not just significance. You can compute the expected loss of shipping B and stop once it drops below a threshold you can tolerate.

When frequentist still wins

If you need a result that regulators or skeptical stakeholders will accept without debating your prior, a pre-registered frequentist test is easier to defend. It is also the default in most tools, such as statsmodels and Optimizely's classic engine.

A practical rule is to use Bayesian when you need to make a call with limited data, and frequentist when you need a result nobody can argue with.

How do I pick a prior without biasing the result?

Start with a weakly informative prior, for example a Beta(1, 1) for conversion rates, which treats every rate as equally likely. If you have historical data, fit a Beta to past conversion rates and then widen it, because a prior that is too confident will drown out the new experiment.

Then run a sensitivity check. Re-run the analysis with two or three different priors, and if the decision flips, you do not have enough data yet to trust any of them.
